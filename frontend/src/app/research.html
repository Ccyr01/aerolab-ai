Research question & experimental conditions

Investigating the relationship between surface microphone measurements, DBD actuator forcing frequency, and aerodynamic lift through experimental data and engineered signal features.

The research question

Can surface microphone measurements improve our understanding and prediction of aerodynamic lift beyond knowing the DBD actuator forcing frequency alone?

Experimental conditions

DBD forcing frequencies span 1–20 Hz, with 0 Hz representing the uncontrolled baseline. Experiments were conducted at a chord-based Reynolds number of approximately 50,000.

REPORTED FINDINGS · FIGURE 6 & SECTION IV.D

Microphone Feature Importance

Permutation importance, evaluated on held-out cross-validation data, identified informative microphone measurements for predicting lift coefficient (CL) and normalized lift improvement (R).

MIC7 · LIFT COEFFICIENT (CL)

Spectral features led the ranking

Mic7 was particularly informative for predicting CL. The highest-ranked features, in reported order, were:

  1. Spectral energy in the 50–150 Hz band
  2. Spectral energy in the 150–300 Hz band
  3. Spectral centroid

MIC7 · ADDITIONAL INFORMATIVE FEATURES

Signal amplitude and shape

These Mic7 measurements also helped predict lift coefficient:

  • Peak-to-peak amplitude: the signal's overall amplitude range
  • RMS: a measure of signal magnitude
  • Kurtosis: a measure of the signal distribution's tail behavior

MIC7 + MIC10 · NORMALIZED LIFT IMPROVEMENT (R)

Information across microphones

Important spectral-energy features came from both Mic7 and Mic10 when predicting normalized lift improvement. Other microphones also contributed.

The reported findings highlight contributions from multiple microphone locations.

What does permutation importance mean?

Shuffling a feature's values reveals how much the model relies on it for accurate predictions. A larger drop in accuracy on held-out data indicates greater reliance on that feature.

Importance indicates predictive association, not causation. An informative microphone feature does not establish that it causes a change in lift.
